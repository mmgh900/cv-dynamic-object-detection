\section{Approach}

The pipeline localises the dynamic object in the first frame of each
input sequence using three classical building blocks: sparse
\textbf{SIFT} features, nearest-neighbour descriptor matching with
\textbf{Lowe's ratio test}, and a displacement-based classifier. A
careful look at the sequences shows that the camera is
\emph{essentially static} in four out of five categories (bird, frog,
sheep, squirrel) and shows only a slight pan for the car sequence, so
a full epipolar / homography machinery is not warranted; per-keypoint
displacement is the more honest signal. Each stage lives in a
dedicated source file.

\paragraph{(1) SIFT detection and description.}
For every input frame we compute \texttt{cv::SIFT::create(1500)} and
run \texttt{detectAndCompute}, producing up to $1500$ keypoints and
their $128$-D descriptors. SIFT is invariant to scale and rotation
and fairly tolerant to illumination changes, which is important for
these outdoor sequences.

\paragraph{(2) Descriptor matching with Lowe's ratio.}
Between frame $0$ and every subsequent frame $f$ we run
\texttt{cv::BFMatcher(NORM\_L2)} with \texttt{knnMatch($k{=}2$)} and
apply \textbf{Lowe's ratio test}: a match is kept only if
$d_1 < 0.75\,d_2$, where $d_1,d_2$ are the distances to the nearest and
second-nearest descriptors. This discards descriptors whose best
match is ambiguous and is the canonical way to filter matches.

\paragraph{(3) Displacement-based dynamic classification.}
For every frame-$0$ keypoint $i$ we accumulate, over all frames where
the ratio test succeeded, its displacement
$d_{if} = \|p_i^{(f)}-p_i^{(0)}-\bar m^{(f)}\|$, where $\bar m^{(f)}$
is the \emph{median} displacement across all matched keypoints in
that frame. Subtracting the median absorbs any constant-translation
camera motion (the small car pan) without fitting a parametric model.
The per-track score is the average $\bar d_i = \frac{1}{n_i}\sum_f d_{if}$.
We then compute the median $m$ of the $\bar d_i$ distribution,
treat it as the background-motion scale, and keep as \emph{dynamic}
the top $15\%$ of tracks that also satisfy $\bar d_i > \max(6,\,3m)$\,px.
This relative cut-off adapts to very different scene dynamics without
a hand-picked threshold.

\paragraph{(4) Clustering and bounding box.}
The surviving dynamic keypoints on frame $0$ are grouped with a
DBSCAN-like proximity clustering (radius $\varepsilon{=}0.08\cdot
\text{image diagonal}$, $\text{minPts}{=}3$). The heaviest cluster
seeds the box; neighbouring clusters within $0.6\,\varepsilon$ are
merged to recover objects whose texture fragments into parts (e.g.\
head and body of a sheep). Within the merged cluster we run a
\textbf{median-absolute-deviation (MAD)} outlier rejection ---
keypoints farther than $3\,\mathrm{MAD}$ from the median in either
axis are discarded --- then compute the bounding rectangle and pad it
by $1/6$ of its size to cover silhouette pixels that SIFT tends to
miss.

\paragraph{Evaluator.} Reads the ground-truth
\texttt{xmin ymin xmax ymax} file, computes IoU per category, writes a
predicted-box text file and three overlays (tracks, boxes, full).

\section{Discussion of Robustness}

\paragraph{Camera motion.} Four of five sequences are filmed with a
static camera; the car shows a small pan. Our median-flow
compensation in stage~3 explicitly absorbs any constant translation,
which is why the \texttt{car} result is the strongest at IoU $0.78$:
background matches all contribute the same displacement vector,
median-subtraction zeroes them out, and only the car stands out.

\paragraph{Illumination changes.} SIFT descriptors are normalised for
contrast and clipped ($\mathcal L_2$-normalise, clip at $0.2$,
re-normalise), so moderate brightness or contrast changes do not
destroy matches. This is visible in \texttt{bird} and \texttt{frog}
where dappled light on leaves does not leak into the dynamic set.

\paragraph{Occlusion.} An object that is temporarily occluded simply
fails to match in a few frames; because our classifier averages over
\emph{all} successful matches, a track that disappears for $20\%$ of
the sequence still votes correctly. This is what lets us detect the
sheep behind the wire fence.

\paragraph{Small / weakly textured objects.} This is the clear failure
mode. For \texttt{squirrel} the object is only $23{\times}31$\,px in a
$259{\times}327$ image and its fur has few stable SIFT extrema;
only $\sim\!13$ tracks end up tagged dynamic and a few of them are
mismatches on nearby leaves, which enlarge the box. For
\texttt{sheep} the issue is different: multiple sheep plus the fence
produce highly repetitive descriptors, so Lowe's ratio rejects many
legitimate matches and the cluster is under-covered. Improvements
strictly within classical CV would be: (i)~running SIFT at a higher
resolution, (ii)~using the keypoint scale to grow the box, or
(iii)~refining the rectangle with edge / colour cues (e.g.\ Canny +
morphological closing inside the predicted region).

\paragraph{Sensitivity analysis.} The two parameters that mattered
most were Lowe's ratio and the selection percentile. Lowe $=0.75$
is the classical setting and we kept it; $0.70$ was too strict for
the textureless bird/frog and $0.80$ let background noise in.
Displacement percentile $15\%$ balanced recall and precision:
$10\%$ missed fragmented objects (sheep), $20\%$ pulled in too many
false positives on the horizon in the car sequence.

\section{Project Delivery}

\paragraph{Code layout.} \texttt{src/feature\_tracker.cpp}
(SIFT + BFMatcher + Lowe), \texttt{src/motion\_segmenter.cpp}
(displacement classifier), \texttt{src/bbox\_estimator.cpp}
(clustering, MAD rejection, box), \texttt{src/evaluator.cpp}
(IoU/metrics), \texttt{src/main.cpp} (entry point),
\texttt{include/dod.hpp}, \texttt{CMakeLists.txt}. Build with
\texttt{cmake -S . -B build \&\& cmake --build build}; run with
\texttt{./build/detect dataset output}. OpenCV~4 is the only
dependency; no deep-learning components are used.

\paragraph{Contribution.} Single-author submission (Mahdi Gheysari):
all ideas, implementation, tuning, evaluation, and report.
\textbf{Working hours: $\approx 18$\,h} (design 2\,h, first
KLT+RANSAC iteration 5\,h, rewrite to SIFT-based approach to match
the course 4\,h, tuning/debugging 4\,h, report and figures 3\,h).
