\section{Our approach}

The project asks us to find one moving object in a short sequence
of images and draw a box around it on the first frame. The brief
suggests using sparse local features and either feature matching or
optical flow, so we follow that path.

We watched the videos before writing any code. In four of the five
sequences (bird, frog, sheep, squirrel) the camera does not move at
all. In the car sequence the camera does a small pan from left to
right. So the trick is simple: if we follow some points across the
frames, the points on the background stay still (or all move
together with the camera) and the points on the object move
differently. We just need to spot which points are different.

\paragraph{What we tried first.} We started with SIFT features and
matched them between frame 0 and every later frame using Lowe's
ratio test. It worked for the car and the sheep, but it failed
badly on the squirrel: the squirrel is very small and its fur does
not give SIFT enough stable points, so we got an IoU of $0.31$
which is below the threshold. We switched to optical flow because
the brief allows it and because optical flow does not need a
``perfect'' point, just a corner that the tracker can follow.

\paragraph{What we do now.} The pipeline has six short steps:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item Apply local histogram equalisation (CLAHE, clip $0.5$,
  $8\times8$ tiles) to every frame. This brightens dim, low-contrast
  regions like the frog's smooth back so the next step can find
  corners there. The clip limit is small on purpose: we want a
  gentle lift, not amplified noise on already-good areas.
\item Find good corners on the equalised first frame
  (\texttt{cv::goodFeaturesToTrack}).
\item Follow each corner from frame to frame using pyramidal
  Lucas--Kanade optical flow
  (\texttt{cv::cal\-cOpticalFlowPyrLK}). We also run the flow
  backwards as a sanity check and drop any track that does not
  return to where it started.
\item For each frame, take the median displacement of all the
  surviving tracks and call it the camera. Subtract it. What is
  left is real object motion.
\item For each track we get an average leftover motion. We sort the
  tracks and keep the top $15\%$, but only if their motion is
  bigger than $\max(8\,\text{px}, 4\times\text{median})$. The floor
  is what removes leaves moving in the wind, walkers in the
  background, and the nearly-still sheep that the brief tells us to
  ignore.
\item Group the surviving points by distance, take the biggest
  group, throw away points that are too far from the centre, and
  return a slightly padded bounding box. A small extra step uses
  pixel differences to shrink the box if it is clearly too big, but
  it only kicks in when the new box is much smaller than the old
  one.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Robustness}

\paragraph{Camera motion.} Four sequences have a still camera, the
car has a small pan. We do not try to fit a fancy camera model;
subtracting the median displacement is enough to cancel a constant
shift, and it is what makes the car case work.

\paragraph{Lighting.} Optical flow is sensitive to brightness
changes, but the forward--backward check kills tracks where the
``motion'' is really an illumination artefact. The dappled light on
leaves in the bird and frog sequences does not leak into the
moving set.

\paragraph{Occlusion.} If a track gets covered for a few frames the
flow simply fails for those frames and the track is dropped
temporarily. We average over the frames that did succeed, so a
short occlusion does not destroy the score.

\paragraph{Sensitivity.} Two parameters mattered. The corner
quality level $0.005$ is permissive enough to find corners on
weakly textured surfaces (like squirrel fur) without flooding the
background; $0.01$ lost the squirrel and $0.001$ added grass false
positives on the car. The displacement percentile $15\%$ balanced
recall and precision: $10\%$ missed pieces of the sheep, $20\%$
brought in horizon false positives on the car.

\section{Why it works on every sequence}

\paragraph{Bird, car, sheep --- the easy cases.} These three
objects all have lots of strong corners on them: the bird has dark
edgy feathers against bright leaves, the car has hard metal lines,
the sheep has black legs and a fence. Lucas--Kanade follows these
corners well across the whole sequence, the moving ones stand out
clearly from the still background, and the cluster covers most of
the object. Three easy true positives.

\paragraph{Squirrel --- where optical flow saves us.} The squirrel
is only about $1\%$ of the image, so it is the hardest case. SIFT
gave up on it (IoU $0.31$, our only failure with SIFT) because
SIFT wants stable scale-space extrema and the fur does not give
any. Shi--Tomasi is happier: it just needs a corner with two
strong gradient directions, and the squirrel's eyes, ears and tree
contact points give a few of those. We get only five moving
tracks but they all sit on the squirrel, so the box is tight and
correct (IoU $0.62$).

\paragraph{Frog --- where CLAHE saves us.} Without CLAHE the frog
was our only failure (IoU $0.28$). Its body is wet and smooth,
almost no local gradient, so Shi--Tomasi found barely any corners
on the body itself --- only on the limbs and the branch next to
it. The cluster covered the limbs only and the box was too tight.
After we add CLAHE the dim greenish skin gets a local contrast
boost, Shi--Tomasi places a handful of corners along the back and
sides too, the cluster covers the whole body, and the box reaches
IoU $0.53$. This is the fix Riccardo suggested when he saw the
failure.

\paragraph{What we could still try.} Grow the cluster bounding box
by the spread of dynamic corners (so a tight cluster would still
expand to a plausible object size), or detect new corners
periodically instead of only on frame 0. Both are nice-to-haves
now that all five sequences are true positives.


\section{Code and credit}

\paragraph{Code layout.} \texttt{src/feature\_tracker.cpp}
(corners + Lucas--Kanade + median-flow),
\texttt{motion\_segmenter.cpp} (displacement classifier),
\texttt{bbox\_estimator.cpp} (clustering and bounding box),
\texttt{silhouette\_refiner.cpp} (optional shrink),
\texttt{evaluator.cpp} (IoU), \texttt{main.cpp} (entry point),
\texttt{include/dod.hpp}, \texttt{CMakeLists.txt}. Build with
\texttt{cmake -S . -B build \&\& cmake --build build}; run with
\texttt{./build/detect dataset output}. OpenCV~4 is the only
dependency. No deep learning.
